Recount Variance Report
RECOUNT | PRIMARY ELECTION | JULY 30, 2024
SYNOPSIS:
Pursuant to A.R.S. § 16-661 through A.R.S. § 16-664 and the 2023 version of the Elections Procedures Manual, Maricopa
County has completed the reconciliation of the Automatic Recount for the United States Representative Congressional                ‘
District 3 Democratic (CD3-DEM) contest. All early and provisional ballots were included in the recount. The Election Day
ballots from all Vote Centers where a voter that was eligible to vote a ballot with the CD3-DEM contest were included.               |
This included 131 of the 222 total Vote Centers from the Primary Election. During the recount, there were 3,664 batches
tabulated, totaling 705,034 ballots. This included 44,036 ballots cast in the CD3-DEM contest. Below is a summary of the
variances identified during the recount in Maricopa County.              '                       .
« Ballot Variance —The recount identified two separate ballot variances from the original count. Both ballot
variances involved a ballot that was jammed as it was being fed into the tabulator during the original count.

o Oneinstance happened at a Vote Center, involving a ballot that was inadvertently fed through a tabulator
a second time after it had been counted during a paper jam. The voter under the guidance of the poll               §
worker reinserted the ballot into the tabulator not realizing it had already been counted when it jammed.

This resulted in one less Election Day ballot being reported in the recount and one less vote for Wooten.

o A second instance occurred on a central count tabulator. When counting a batch of early ballots, one
ballot was not tabulated in the original count after a batch of ballots jammed as they were being fed into
the tabulator. This resulted in one more early ballot being reported in the recount and an additional vote
for Raquel Teran.

« Adjudication Variance — Federal and state law require adjudication boards to tally results for write-in candidates,
overvotes, blank ballots and ambiguous marks (e.g. stray marks, check marks). The recount adjudication boards                  |
confirmed the decisions made by the original adjudication in nearly all instances. There were 2 instances that
impacted the recount resuits where the recount adjudication board came to a different conclusion than the             ;
original count adjudication board. Both instances are described below.                                                               |

o Oneinstance occurred when the adjudication board from the original count determined that the CD3-DEM                     E
contest was overvoted. During the recount, the board determined that the contest was not overvoted and                    f
removed an overvote, awarding a vote to Raquel Teran. Upon inspection of the ballot, the voter had a              !
consistent marking pattern and used black ink throughout the ballot. In the CD3- DEM contest the voter
filled in a second oval and appeared to use white-out or another substance that turned the ink to a light
blue and placed a shimmer over the oval. This resulted in Raquel Terédn gaining a vote in the recount as
compared to the original count.

o The second instance occurred when a voter partially filled in the ovals next to Raquel Terdn and the write-
in line. The voter then wrote in Raquel Teran’s nhame in the write-in line. The original adjudication board
removed the overvote, but failed to award the vote to Raquel Terén as indicated by the voter's intent. The
recount adjudication board, correctly assighed the vote to Raquel Teran. This resulted in Raquel Terdn
gaining a vote in the recount as compared to the original count.
