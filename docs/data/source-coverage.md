# Source coverage

## Meaning and scope

This is an evidence inventory, not a claim that every locked item is published. A source-lock entry is byte-identified input, extract, artifact, contract, or planning material; `retained` means its recorded path is in the repository, while `nonretained` means only its identity, size, and hash are locked. The current lock has **1,055 entries: 1,015 retained and 40 nonretained**. Connecticut's ten endorsement-certificate bundles and statewide 2024 Democratic primary candidate list are nonretained because the official PDFs contain residential addresses; their minimum factual observations, proposed current-identity and historical-geography links, and joint reviewer queue are retained in privacy-filtered artifacts. The official 2022 and 2024 general-election Statements of Vote, a ten-row nominee/result cross-check receipt, four cycle-specific official nomination-statute instruments, and a two-cycle conditional statutory-authority receipt are retained separately. A v2 reviewer package joins those evidence layers without resolving primary nomination trigger facts, approving records, or enabling scoring/publication. Virginia's official Census CD118 authority, twelve-row current-incumbent identity candidate, twelve-row geography candidate, and joint reviewer queue are retained separately. Verify the complete ledger with `npm run data:verify` ([`data/source-lock.json`](../../data/source-lock.json)).

Coverage records use an explicit denominator and status (`complete`, `partial`, or an explicit missing/unavailable judgment) for a named release and domain; they do not turn a source lock into publication. `candidate-only` means a disposable or unpublished candidate was validated and is not public. `published` below means the R1 identity/geography release only. Missing credentials, a landing page, unreviewed material, or an absent lock entry means `not_collected` or `unassessed` as applicable—not `source_unavailable` or `license_unavailable`. Those reasons require a versioned assessment.
